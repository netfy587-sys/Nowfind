Six careers, verified

The players whose records the sport still measures itself against.

Achievements only, as a matter of record — not photographs of the athletes themselves.

Lin Dan

China · Men's singles · Retired 2020

The only men's singles player to complete the "Super Grand Slam" — all nine major titles in world badminton — reaching it by age 28. He also became the first man to win back-to-back Olympic singles golds, in 2008 and 2012.

2× Olympic gold5× World champion239 weeks at No.1

Lee Chong Wei

Malaysia · Men's singles · Retired 2019

Held the world No.1 ranking for a record 349 consecutive weeks, yet never won an Olympic or World Championship title — finishing runner-up to Lin Dan at three separate Olympic finals, arguably the sport's cruellest rivalry.

3× Olympic silver349-week No.1 streak69 career titles

Viktor Axelsen

Denmark · Men's singles · Active

The first non-Asian man to win Olympic singles gold since 1996, then the second player ever — after Lin Dan — to defend that Olympic title, winning at both Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024.

2× Olympic gold2× World champion4× European champion

PV Sindhu

India · Women's singles · Active

The first Indian woman to win two individual Olympic medals — silver at Rio 2016, bronze at Tokyo 2020 — and the first Indian, of either gender, to win a BWF World Championship singles title, in 2019.

Olympic silver + bronzeWorld champion 20195× World medallist

Carolina Marín

Spain · Women's singles · Active

The only European to ever win an Olympic singles badminton title, taking gold at Rio 2016 in a sport almost entirely dominated by Asian nations at the top level — and backed it up with three World Championship titles.

Olympic gold 20163× World champion6× European champion

Tai Tzu-ying

Taiwan · Women's singles · Active

Spent longer at world No.1 than any other women's singles player in BWF history, built almost entirely on deceptive, unorthodox shot-making rather than power — a genuinely different template for how the position could be played.

Most of these records were built on losses, not just wins.

Lee Chong Wei's three Olympic finals, Tai Tzu-ying's missing Olympic gold — badminton's most quoted careers often include exactly the title that got away, which is part of why they're remembered as closely as the ones that didn't.
